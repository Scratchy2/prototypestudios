DLM() Color Picker

The new way to represent colors with three values: D, L, and M. They stand for Dynamic, Luminance, and Mix.
Try it out by hovering over and clicking on the CIE 1931 generated from the official observer data below or typing in the textboxes to convert your colors to DLM.
Note: the bigger your screen is, the longer it takes to draw the canvas. A computer is recommended.

Triangles

These checkboxes toggle the display of the triangle coverage of three of the most popular color gamuts: sRGB (standard since 1996), Display-P3 (more modern and common in Apple devices), and Rec2020 (a wide, future-proof gamut).



Luminance

This textbox controls one specific value in the CIE 1931 that roughly controls brightness when normalized to cone responses: Y. The minimum is 0 (no light), and the maximum is 1 (full light).


Color

These give you basic information about the color you clicked on the CIE 1931 diagram above. "dominant" is a shortened way of saying "dominant wavelength," and this mirrors the information you see when hovering over the diagram. You can also view the color you clicked on.

x: 0.3127

y: 0.3290

dominant: 0 nm


DLM()

This is how you can represent that same exact color using the three values D, L, and M instead of R, G, and B as well as a breakdown of each part.

dlm(493.11910, 100.00000%, 59.48544%)

Dynamic: 493.11910 nm

Luminance: 100.00000%

Mix: 59.48544%

Converter

Click on a textbox to begin typing in your hex, RGB, color(), or dlm() color according to the placeholder. When you have finished inputting your color, click anywhere outside of that textbox to watch everything become converted to DLM().

would any of this work if you viewed it on a DLM() device?

assuming your color gamut is "dlm" and your hardware comes with a dynamic laser + a 650 nm laser, this website will display accurate colors with dlm() on the preview and the tooltip, but not the CIE 1931. the CIE 1931 will display in rec2020 and internally be converted to DLM() because DLM() is larger than rec2020, display-p3, and srgb.
